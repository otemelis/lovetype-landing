Conflict style pairing

Processor and Pursuer.

One partner needs to talk it out now; the other needs hours or a day.

Conflict style describes what a person does when a disagreement gets difficult. It is a tendency, not a fault, and no combination is doomed.

The two styles

Processor: Needs time before responding to conflict, so what they say is considered rather than reactive. The deepest insights arrive later than the argument.

Pursuer: Needs to address a problem now. Letting tension sit unresolved hurts more than the conflict itself, and a partner going quiet can feel like withdrawn love.

Where you connect

The pursuer will not let it fester; the processor will give a thoughtful answer once they have had time. Bridge the timing gap and the pursuer gets the resolution they need while the processor gets to give a response they stand behind.

Where the friction shows up

The pursuer needs to talk it out now; the processor needs hours or a day. The silence spikes the pursuer's anxiety, the pushing makes the processor retreat further, and it can end in a standoff where neither need is met.

What each of you can try

If you are the processor partnerGive them a landing point immediately — 'I need until tonight, and I promise we'll finish this.' The specific commitment is what lets them stop chasing.
If you are the pursuer partnerTheir pause is not rejection — it is how they avoid saying something careless. Hold the anxiety for the few hours it takes and you will get a better conversation.
A pattern, not a verdictStyles describe tendencies, not destinies. Plenty of couples with this pairing do well; knowing the pattern is what makes it workable.

Questions

One of us needs to talk immediately and the other needs to think — how do we meet?

With a concrete commitment. 'I need until tonight and then we finish this' gives the pursuer something to hold onto, which lets them stop chasing, which lets the processor actually think.
