Conflict style pairing

Defender and Pursuer.

Neither partner buries a conflict — but the volume can climb fast.

Conflict style describes what a person does when a disagreement gets difficult. It is a tendency, not a fault, and no combination is doomed.

The two styles

Defender: When they feel criticised or unfairly blamed, defends their case first. Can hear feedback — but not until they’ve had the chance to explain themselves.

Pursuer: Needs to address a problem now. Letting tension sit unresolved hurts more than the conflict itself, and a partner going quiet can feel like withdrawn love.

Where you connect

Neither partner buries a conflict. The pursuer will not let it drop; the defender will not back down until heard. Things get aired — nothing festers for months in this pairing.

Where the friction shows up

The pursuer chases resolution while the defender is still making their case, and the volume climbs on both sides. The defender feels steamrolled toward a conclusion; the pursuer feels like their partner is fighting the process instead of solving the problem.

What each of you can try

If you are the defender partnerAcknowledge their need for reassurance even mid-argument — 'we're okay, I'm just not done explaining.' It lowers the temperature enough for both of you to think.
If you are the pursuer partnerLet them finish before you push for the resolution. They cannot meet you at the end if they do not feel heard at the start.
A pattern, not a verdictStyles describe tendencies, not destinies. Plenty of couples with this pairing do well; knowing the pattern is what makes it workable.

Questions

Why do our arguments escalate so fast?

Because one of you is pushing for a resolution while the other is still trying to be understood, and each reads the other's move as the problem. Slowing down — reassurance from one side, patience from the other — takes the heat out.
