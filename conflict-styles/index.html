Conflict styles.How you handle tension.

Pursuer, withdrawer, fixer, peacekeeper, defender, processor: what each conflict style does under tension and how the pairings play out.

Take the conflict style test
In shortA conflict style is what a person tends to do when a disagreement with a partner gets difficult. LoveType describes six: the pursuer (needs to resolve it now), the withdrawer (needs space first), the fixer (goes straight to solutions), the peacekeeper (smooths things over), the defender (explains their side first) and the processor (needs time to think). None is wrong; trouble usually comes from how two styles collide, such as the well-known pursue–withdraw loop.

Conflict styles: questions

What is the pursuer–withdrawer pattern?

It is the loop where one partner pushes to talk during conflict and the other pulls away for space, and each move makes the other stronger. It eases when the withdrawer names a time to come back and the pursuer can let the pause happen.

Is one conflict style healthier than the others?

No. Each style protects something real: the connection, the relationship, fairness or clarity. What matters is whether both partners can repair after a fight.
